What the model learns about epistasis, and how to measure it

A review of the epistasis hierarchy, the assay's saturation, the mechanisms that are meant to learn each level, and a refreshed set of 20 validation metrics with the evidence behind each one. Every number here comes from the capped split's validation libraries, from validation dumps of eleven past runs, or from simulation on the same validation design.

Summary

Variance

Of the variance of measured dddG on the validation doubles, 56% is a function of the additive expectation alone (saturation and other nonspecific epistasis), 22% is position-pair offsets, 14% line effects and 6% the specific residue combination. The last two are about the size of measurement noise. The training-set ANOVA agrees (60 / 16 / 13 / 10%, cross-validated).

Saturation

It leaks into every level of raw dddG, because on the observed scale the floor makes even an additive model non-additive. A predictor that knows only the saturation scores 0.58 on the naive dddG correlation in simulation, more than one that knows every epistasis component but not the saturation (0.42). Two devices remove it exactly for an additive model: within-column order of ddG_AB, and residuals beyond each side's own global curve. Doubles whose additive expectation lies below the floor carry almost no specific information. Their dddG is the floor minus x, plus a rescue signal.

The floor is misplaced

Doubles far below the floor are measured near dG +0.3 on average (median +0.03 across 61 libraries, range -0.55 to +0.70), not at the link's -1. Every trained model under-predicts the nonspecific epistasis by 0.5 to 0.7 kcal/mol. The link alone reproduces about a third of the measured global curve, and the MT head carries part of the rest as nonspecific latent epistasis.

Metrics

The new set is 17 epistasis metrics, one or two per level with controls, plus 3 ddG guards. It replaces about 60 aggregate series. Each level's metric credits only its own component in simulation (for example cell_rank: 0.29 when the interaction is known, at most 0.012 otherwise). On all 67 real dumps the additive control scores exactly 0 at every residual level and within 0.002 of 0 at the rank level.

Stop on

val_epi_cell_rank_mt, confirmed by cell_mag_comb and cell_flipacc_mt (r 0.80 to 0.83 among them). Their noise between replicate runs is 0.005, 0.007 and 0.010, and training moves them by 8 to 12 times that. An average of the three, each scaled by its replicate noise, separates configurations best (spread 3.4 replicate SDs).

Runs so far

The released baseline (no link, MT rank 16) leads every devel arm at the cell level at matched epoch 2 (cell_rank_mt 0.197 vs 0.146 to 0.167, 6 to 11 replicate SDs). The no-link devel arm is no better than the link arms, so the link is not the cause; MT capacity is the leading suspect, which the queued o6h2_mt16 arm tests. In the devel arms the MT adapter adds only about +0.02 over the WT-in-context control on cell_rank.

Objectives

The data contain 27,000 confident order reversals (2 x 2 sub-blocks whose within-column order flips by more than 0.6 kcal/mol on both sides) in the training matrices. Each fixes the sign of a pure interaction contrast and is immune to saturation, which makes a logistic loss on them a candidate interaction-only objective. Separately, the floor misplacement argues for censoring measured values near the practical floor in the MT regression, or for a higher, library-aware floor.

The hierarchy and its variance

A double mutant carries residue a at position i and b at j (i < j) in a library whose wild type has stability c = dG_wt. It has a measured ddG_AB, both measured singles, and the epistasis score dddG = ddG_AB − ddG_A − ddG_B. Its additive expectation x = c + ddG_A + ddG_B is the dG the double would have if the substitutions did not interact. All doubles of one position pair form a matrix, rows a and columns b (19 × 19 when complete; the 24 validation matrices are 324 to 361 cells), so dddG divides into four nested levels:

The levels are additive in variance when each is computed on what the level above leaves. On the validation doubles (in-sample, complete blocks), and in the earlier training-set ANOVA (127,476 doubles, cross-validated so noise fitted by row and column means is not counted as explained):

What the noise leaves to learn

Measurement noise is about 0.2 to 0.27 kcal/mol per variant (replicate rows of the same mutant give 0.27; protease disagreement 0.22 to 0.31). Three facts follow, and they set the ceiling for every metric below:

  • The cell level is at the noise floor. A double's own noise, double-centred over a 19 × 19 block, contributes 0.9σ², which is 0.036 to 0.066. The measured cell variance is 0.040. The specific interaction is therefore a small share of the variance (about 0 to 5%, most likely about 2%, in the training ANOVA). It is still real: trained models get the direction of 80 to 88% of confident order reversals right, and the measured reversals are far more frequent than noise alone produces.
  • Most of the line level is the singles' noise. dddG subtracts the measured ddG_A from every cell of A's line, so that single's error becomes a line effect of variance σ² (0.04 to 0.07). The measured line variance is 0.037 (rows) and 0.053 (columns). A model cannot predict this part on new proteins, so line metrics are attenuated by it, not biased.
  • The pair level is mostly library-level. 70% of the variance of the 24 pair offsets is between the 9 libraries, so the pair level has about 9 independent units in validation. It can be reported, not optimised.

Where saturation matters, and where it does not

The assay reports dG through a monotone, saturating response h: it cannot report much below its floor, where unfolded variants are pinned, or above its ceiling. For a double, write the latent epistasis as e. Then

dddG_measured = [ h(c+a+b) − h(c+a) − h(c+b) + h(c) ] + [ h(c+a+b+e) − h(c+a+b) ] + noise saturation term S: a function of the epistasis, passed through the local singles only, non-zero wherever h curves slope h′: attenuated where h is flat

Saturation therefore acts in two ways. It adds a term that depends only on the singles and is large wherever the double's additive expectation reaches the curved part of h. It also hides real epistasis where h is flat. How much of each depends on where the double sits:

"Practical floor": the median measured dG of the double. The last two columns are Spearman correlations with measured dddG inside the regime, for the saturation-only control (the WT head through the link) and for the trained model.

Where saturation has a meaningful effect

  • Doubles below the floor (x < −1; 18% of validation doubles, 34% of the sum of squares of dddG). The measured double sits near the practical floor (median dG +0.29), so dddG ≈ floor − x: a deterministic function of the singles. These doubles carry almost no information about which residues interact. The exception is rescue: a double measured well above the floor despite x below it, which is real compensation (an earlier analysis put a third of such doubles in this class). Every statistic on raw dddG weights these doubles most heavily, because their dddG is largest.
  • The knee (−1 < x < 1, 45%). The saturation term is partial and the attenuation of real epistasis is partial; both depend on the library's floor.
  • Any statistic on raw dddG, at any level. On the observed scale, h(c+a+b) is not additive in a and b, so saturation survives centring by pair, by line and even double-centring. The old raw metrics gave the saturation-only control 0.50 (pair), 0.53 (line) and 0.26 (double-centred cell) on real validation data, and its residue-pair table ("identity effect") 0.6.
  • Ranking dddG (not ddG_AB) inside a column. dddG subtracts ddG_A, which varies along a column, so its order is not monotone-invariant. The old "ranked interaction" metric gave the saturation-only control 0.09.

Where it does not

  • The order of doubles inside one column. Same library, same partner, one monotone h: the order of ddG_AB is the order of the latent stabilities. This is exact. Pinned cells at the floor randomise the order among themselves, which only attenuates. The cell-rank metric and the confident-reversal accuracy are built on it.
  • Residuals beyond each side's own global curve. Subtracting E[dG_AB | x] from the measurement and each head's own additive prediction (through its own link) from the prediction removes the saturation term for an additive model exactly. The magnitude metrics are built on it.
  • Doubles comfortably in range (x > 1). The link is near-linear there, and the link-only control predicts almost no dddG (0.06 kcal/mol). But "in range" is not "trend-free": measured dddG still falls from +0.5 at x = 1.5 to 0 above x = 2.5, and inside the bin the control still correlates 0.24 by rank. Restricting to in-range doubles is therefore not a clean device on its own, and it would discard about two-thirds of the data.

The model's explicit saturation is incomplete. With the link on, the link alone reproduces only about a third of the measured global curve below the floor (0.85 of 2.3 kcal/mol at x ≈ −2.1). Above x = 0 it reproduces almost none of the remaining trend. The trained model's global bias is −0.5 kcal/mol, and the MT pass alone (which carries the non-additive part) shows a smaller bias (−0.28) than the combined head. The MT head is learning part of the nonspecific curve as latent epistasis, which is capacity spent on the assay rather than on residue-specific coupling. Without a link the bias is larger (−0.65 to −0.68).

The metric set

Twenty numbers per validation, plus the ddG product per library. Each epistasis metric is computed once, on the doubles of every validation library pooled, with the pair matrices as units (src/esm_msr/epi_metrics.py). Four heads give each metric a prediction to score and the controls it needs:

ctx control(WT + ~WT)/2what the backbone plus single-mutant training already know; the MT adapter's contribution is mt (or comb) minus ctx. Needs --val_cycle_passes, now on by default (about 1 to 2 minutes per validation)

How the two devices work

Beyond-global residuals carry the magnitude metrics. The measured residual is dG_AB − E[dG_AB | x], which equals dddG − E[dddG | x] (a 25-bin piecewise-linear smoother on the measured x). For a head, the residual is its observed-scale double minus its own additive prediction pushed through its own link, e = P_AB − h(c + L_A + L_B + o) + c (o is the head's median second difference, which removes a calibration bias), minus E[e | x̂] on the head's own additive expectation. Because each side is measured against its own global curve, a head that is additive before its link has e = 0 and scores exactly 0. That holds whatever its link does to the singles and whether or not there is a link. Pair offsets, line effects and double-centred cells are then taken from these residuals.

Within-column order carries the rank metrics. cell_rank rank-transforms every column of measured ddG_AB and of the head's latent ddG_AB on a complete block, double-centres both rank matrices and correlates them (both orientations, mean per block, mean over 24 blocks). cell_flipacc takes every 2 × 2 sub-block (two substitutions × two partners of one matrix) whose measured order reverses by more than 0.6 kcal/mol on both sides, 2,509 of them in validation. It scores the share in which the head's latent contrast Lij − Li′j − Lij′ + Li′j′ has the reversal's sign. Pair, line and single effects cancel exactly in that contrast, so the score is 0.5 for any additive head. A monotone assay cannot reverse an order, so the measured side is saturation-free.

Evidence the metrics isolate their level

The simulation keeps the real validation design: the same 24 matrices, the same dG_wt and the same single effects. It plants pair, line and interaction components (calibrated so the measured variance partition matches Figure 2), measures through a saturating assay with a soft floor and a nonspecific global term, adds noise of 0.2 kcal/mol and censors below −1. Each row below is a predictor that knows the singles with realistic error (SD 0.6) and only the listed components (each with noise). Means of 16 replicates; the replicate SD of the levels used for stopping is 0.01 to 0.02.

Table 3. Shading is the share of the column's top value. "Imitated" saturation: a no-link predictor that reproduces the floor with its own noise (SD 0.25), as a model fitting observed values without a link would. The three right-hand columns are the old raw definitions, shown for contrast. Flip accuracy is 0.5 at chance.

Read across each row: the level metrics credit only their own component, whatever the saturation and with or without a link. The residual leaks are small and known. Flip accuracy gives 0.54 to 0.55 to a predictor whose latent has a nonspecific curvature, because noise reversals happen more often in the floor-compressed column. cell_mag can carry a little of the line level through a sharp floor. cell_rank has neither leak. The naive metric ranks the predictors the wrong way round: knowing only the saturation scores 0.58, while knowing every epistasis component but not the saturation scores 0.42. A no-link model that imitates the saturation loses about half its cell-level skill to its own imitation noise (cell_rank 0.15 vs 0.29).

On real data the controls behave as designed. Across all eleven runs and 67 dumps the additive head scores exactly 0 at every residual level, within 0.002 of 0 on cell_rank (bf16 noise can reorder near-ties) and 0.47 to 0.53 on reversal accuracy, while it scores up to 0.49 on the naive metric whenever a link is on. The new cell-rank metric tracks the old conditional-item flip metric (r = 0.89 across 58 run-epochs) but is higher and has controls.

  3. Believe a single-seed difference only above about 2.8 replicate SDs: cell_rank_mt 0.014, cell_mag 0.02, reversal accuracy 0.03, beyond-global 0.011, line 0.04, pair 0.06. Below that, run a second seed.

  6. Do not optimise naive_rho (under the link it mostly measures the link, and across runs it is uncorrelated or negatively correlated with every clean level), pair_rho (24 pairs, 9 libraries) or cell_sigsd (expressiveness, not skill).

Training objectives and experiments that follow

A confident-reversal loss

Each confident reversal in the training matrices fixes the sign of the latent interaction contrast of four doubles, and nothing else. Pair, line and single effects cancel, the measured side is immune to saturation, and the 0.6 kcal/mol margin keeps noise reversals rare. A logistic loss on the MT pass's latent contrast is therefore an interaction-only objective that uses only the reliable part of the signal:

L_flip = mean over confident reversals (i, i′, j, j′) of softplus( −κ · s · (L_ij − L_i′j − L_ij′ + L_i′j′) ), s = sign(y_ij − y_i′j)

The training matrices hold 27,259 such reversals (0.4% of 6.4 million 2 × 2 sub-blocks; median 30 per oriented matrix). The current sampler puts three columns of a pair in a micro-batch, which holds about 1.4 reversals on average (30% of units hold any). The loss would need either whole matrices per step or a detached memory of the other columns' latent predictions to form the contrasts. Zero-shot, the base model gets 87% of the reversals larger than 1 kcal/mol right and 72% of all those larger than 0.6; trained models reach 93 to 96% and 80 to 88%. Compared with the current MT terms, this loss does not reward the single-effect consensus (the within-column ListMLE does) and does not regress on a target at the noise floor (the interaction component of the MT regression does).

The floor

Doubles far below the floor are measured near dG 0 to +0.5, library-dependently, and the link's floor sits at −1. Two existing switches address it without new code: --censor_floor 0.5 --censor_floor_hinge. These make measured values at or below 0.5 a bound for the MT regression, so neither the link nor the MT head is asked to reproduce pinned values. The alternative is a learned floor initialised near 0, or a per-library floor offset for training libraries. Expected signature: global_bias_comb moves toward 0 through the link (global_err_add falls) rather than through the MT head, and the cell level is unchanged or better.

Experiments, in order of information per GPU-hour

  1. o6h2_mt16 (queued): MT rank 16 / α 16 on devel. If it closes the gap to the baseline on cell_rank_mt, capacity was the limit.
  2. o6h3_colrank0 (queued): no within-column ListMLE. Expected to change the cell level little if the analysis above is right.
  3. Floor censoring under the link: the best of the above plus --censor_floor 0.5 --censor_floor_hinge. Read global_bias_comb, global_err_add and the cell score.
  4. The reversal loss (needs implementation), weighted against the existing MT terms with scripts/grad_share_probe.py.

Each of these should be judged on the cell score with the guards. Run two seeds before believing a difference below 0.03 on cell_rank_mt.

Every validation writes training_logs/<run>/0/val_dump_<tag>.npz with all items' latent scores, the reverse leg, dG_wt and the link. Any dump, past runs included, can be rescored with the current definitions and pair-bootstrap intervals:

PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/epi_from_dump.py training_logs/<run>/0/val_dump_e5.npz [...] --boot 200 --csv out.csv

Absolute values carry the sampling uncertainty of a 24-matrix validation set (the 90% pair-bootstrap interval of cell_rank_mt for the baseline is 0.14 to 0.27); paired comparisons on the same set are an order of magnitude tighter (Table 5).
